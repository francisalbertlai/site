The Closed Loop

The real risk of thinking with a machine, and the one exit.

The advertised fear about working closely with AI is over-involvement — too much time with the machine, too much trust in it. That fear points at the wrong organ. The real risk is quieter and more flattering: the machine holds your ideas plus the archived output of mankind, and you start playing entirely inside that space. Everything it says feels new. Almost none of it is. It is recombination — brilliant, tireless, fluent recombination — of what was already written down. An AI is not drawing from some unknown reservoir of intelligence. It is eating the compendium, and the compendium now includes you.

Left unattended, this becomes a loop that closes without announcing itself. Your questions go in; polished versions of your own assumptions come back; the polish reads as progress. The danger was never dissolving into the machine. It's the space of thinkable thoughts quietly shrinking to the space of already-thought ones — with excellent production values.

The loop has one exit, and it isn't in the software. New information enters through instruments, not conversations. A mass spectrometer is not trained on humanity's output. A bench experiment doesn't recombine; it reports. So does a stranger answering an email, a customer saying no, a market clearing at a price nobody modelled. Anything that touches the world outside the archive is upstream of every AI that will ever be trained on it. The discipline that follows is simple to state: conversation with the machine is for compressing what's known; contact with the world is for learning what isn't. Confusing the two is the whole failure mode. Model output is scaffolding — essential during construction, structural never. Nothing from the loop is true until something outside the loop has weighed in.

What's left for the human, then, isn't knowledge-holding. The compendium is better held by the machine — that argument is over. What the machine cannot supply is the question it wasn't asked, the curiosity that points somewhere unfashionable, the decision that this thread and not that one deserves a year of a life. Direction is not in the training data. Neither is wanting something. Taste is non-delegable — deferring on it erodes the organ that generates it, and the atrophy never announces itself.

Something else is happening to knowledge itself, and it looks like standardization. The compendium was always there — books, libraries, papers, brains — but messy, lossy, mutually untranslated. A book is a hope that readers reconstruct the author's structure. What AI adds is a common representation layer: write something, and it can be checked, translated, and connected against everything else — eventually against every other person's externalized map as well. Whether that future is wonderful or weird is an open question. That it is coming seems less open.

A note on the optimism underneath all this. Full disclosure: this page is written by someone who understands these systems well enough to benefit from them, and bias runs toward what one understands — no pretending otherwise. But the optimism has one autobiographical leg to stand on. The jobs AI is supposed to take — the operator jobs, the processing jobs, the knowledge-plumbing — are not where people live. They're where people make a living. Ask anyone actually in one. Nobody settles their soul into a queue of cases; they settle it into what the queue funds. If the machines take the queue, the loss is smaller than advertised and the freed attention larger. The transition will be confusing and some of it will hurt, and rejecting the whole thing is a coherent human response owed some respect. But the technology under all the noise is a translation layer — and a species that has always failed mostly at the interfaces between minds just acquired a universal adapter. The bet here is that it makes humans more legible to each other, not less.

The grounded position, then, in one line: use the machine for everything except the two things it cannot do — touch the world, and want something. Keep those, and the loop stays open.
