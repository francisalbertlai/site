Conversion. Most human activity reduces to converting energy, money, and time into each other — and most friction lives at the badly-designed conversion points. Fusion is the largest unclaimed one.

Translation. Hard ideas rarely fail because they're hard. They fail at the door — the particular way a particular mind needs to receive them. Years of tutoring suggest the door matters more than the idea. What this skill is ultimately for: open question.

Building before understanding. A startup built from zero coding knowledge made $200 total and taught more than a degree did. Working theory since then: build the thing first, let the building decide the qualifications. Results mixed, method retained.

Perception vs. reality. Most conflicts, examined closely, turn out to be with the map, not the territory. Physics is a good teacher here — it doesn't negotiate, which turns out to be oddly comforting rather than bleak.

Mess. Everything real is chaotic and embarrassing in the middle. This appears to be a feature.

Death. Working near it daily turns out to be a very effective filter for what actually matters. Fewer things than you'd think. More than none.

Lithium-6

Fusion power plants will need enriched lithium-6, and almost nobody makes it. New separation chemistry might change who can. The rabbit hole so far runs through the buyer landscape, the economics of a small cascade, the IP terrain, the regulatory perimeter. The open questions are increasingly commercial ones — who buys first, at what price, under whose permits. Where it goes: unknown. The longer version lives here.
