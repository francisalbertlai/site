The Lithium-6 Project

Fusion's fuel cycle has a missing ingredient. This project is finding out whether a crystal can fix it.

The gap

Fusion runs on a metal nobody sells. Every deuterium-tritium fusion plant has to breed its own tritium by firing neutrons at lithium-6 — and no Western merchant supplier of enriched Li-6 exists today. The old plants used mercury by the megaton and closed decades ago. The depleted lithium-7 left over is scarce too: today's reactors want it for coolant chemistry, tomorrow's molten-salt reactors want tonnes of it. Two starved customers, one process. Australia digs up half the world's lithium; the interesting position is between the rock and the reactor.

The trick

A crystal that prefers the lighter atom. In 2025, the Banerjee lab published a mercury-free route: a tunnel-structured vanadium oxide (ζ-V₂O₅) whose channels bind ⁶Li over ⁷Li — a zero-point-energy effect, about 5.7% enrichment per electrochemical cycle, in water, at a bench (Chem 2025). The same group published the full crystal synthesis recipe a decade earlier, open access (Chem. Sci. 2015). None of the science here is original — that's the point. Replicate first, then measure.

The chain

Li-6 sits upstream of more than fusion. Tritium is bred from lithium-6; helium-3 — the gas quantum computers can't get cold without — is what tritium decays into. ⁶Li → ³H → ³He. Supply the first link and you sit above two more starved markets without touching a reactor or a cryostat.

The experiment

The whole thesis fits on one borrowed bench. Make ~50 grams of the crystal, run it on commercial battery-grade lithium, and let a rented mass spectrometer referee. Four questions: can the crystal be made, does it separate on clean feedstock (the gate), does the residual silver stay locked in the lattice, and how many cycles does it survive. That last number swings the plant model from loss-making to 60%+ margins — so it gets measured, not argued. Cost: roughly $11k in a partner lab. Full bill of materials, bench design, and unit economics exist and are available on request.

Open questions

Plenty, and they're written down. The method has been published once, at gram scale, by its inventors — outsider replication is the first real test. Fusion demand is mostly future demand; this could be early. Lithium-6 is dual-use, so the whole thing gets built in the open, safeguards first. And the field isn't empty: a laser-enrichment startup and a NASDAQ-listed isotope company are circling the same market with heavier physics — read here as validation, not doom.

The ask

Wanted: the technical other half. An electrochemist or materials chemist with hands, judgement, and bench access, who'd rather co-own a strategic problem than consult on one. On the table already: the market thesis, the systems work, and the stubbornness. Scepticism is the first contribution being sought — anyone who's read the two papers and thinks this fails gets the most valuable coffee.

On method

This project was researched and drafted in long collaboration with an AI (Anthropic's Claude) — literature, market maps, models, and most first drafts, including this page's. Every load-bearing claim traces to a cited paper or named source, not to model output, and the experiment above exists because nothing here is true until it's measured. The judgement calls and the responsibility sit with the author.
